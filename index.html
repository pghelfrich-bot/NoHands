LectureFlow

Paste your outline

LectureFlow turns a structured outline into an image-first deck: short annotation labels arranged around a central figure instead of walls of bullets. Paste below, or upload a .md/.txt file. Parsing is lenient — rough outlines are fine.

Outline format

# Deck title
Presenter: name
Date: any text
Design: free-text notes
        (colors / mood)

1. TYPE: title | roadmap |
         section | content |
         takeaway
   HEADLINE: slide heading
   POINTS:
   - short or long point
   - another point
   CALLOUT: a highlighted
            stat or quote
   FIGURE: what the central
           image should show
   NOTES: speaker notes
  • title / roadmap / section / takeaway render dark; content renders light.
  • On content slides, POINTS become short labels placed around the figure and connected to it — long points are compressed and the detail moves to speaker notes.
  • FIGURE seeds the image search for that slide.
  • Slides can also start with ## Heading or be separated by ---.
  • Equations: write LaTeX inline as $...$ or on their own line as $$...$$ — they render as typeset math, so explain each symbol in a nearby POINT or in NOTES.

      Settings

      Keys are stored only in this browser and used directly from your browser to each provider. Openverse and Wikimedia Commons need no key. Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay are free with instant signup.

      Background removal uses a free, unlimited in-browser ML model by default (downloads ~5 MB of weights on first use, then cached) — no key needed and no watermarks. The keys above are only used for an optional HD pass when you Alt-click the cut-out (✂) button. Note: a PhotoRoom sandbox key (prefixed sandbox_) watermarks every result, so it's skipped — use a live key for HD.

      Google Images results aren't pre-filtered by license like the other sources — check usage rights before reusing or sharing an image beyond your own slides. Note: by default a Programmable Search Engine only searches the sites you list — turn on "Search the entire web" in its settings at programmablesearchengine.google.com to get full Google Images breadth.
